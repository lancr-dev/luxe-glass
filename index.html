A little more transparency

Is Liquid Glass the same as glassmorphism?

They share translucency, blur, and depth. In this showcase, Liquid Glass puts more emphasis on a coherent material for controls: fine edge highlights, contextual tint, and fluid changes between interaction states.

Will the design work on mobile?

The layout should adapt around smaller screens: fewer floating elements, comfortable touch targets, and compact navigation. The final website needs testing with its actual content on mobile as well as desktop.

Does every part of the website need to be glass?

No. The strongest results use it selectively. Navigation and contextual controls can use glass while body copy, forms, and detailed information stay on stable surfaces.

Can it match my brand colors?

Yes. Backgrounds, accent colors, typography, and surface tint can all follow your brand. Contrast and readability guide how much color and transparency each component uses.

What happens when blur is unavailable?

A solid or more opaque surface keeps the same content and controls usable. Glass is a visual enhancement; the page should remain understandable without the effect.

Will it look exactly like native Liquid Glass?

This is a web interpretation. CSS can suggest translucency, depth, and light-catching edges, but it does not reproduce a native rendering engine's optical behavior exactly.
